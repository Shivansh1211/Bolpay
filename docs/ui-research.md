# UI pattern research

Reviewed public product documentation and guidance on 2026-11-05. These are pattern references only. No third-party image, logo, icon, font file, or screen layout was downloaded, embedded, or copied. The preview will use BolPay's own palette and shapes.

| Source | Pattern observed | Why it works | Fit for BolPay |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Stripe: payment-success pages](https://stripe.com/resources/more/payment-successful-pages) | Put the payment outcome first, then show the details and the next useful action. | A fast, explicit confirmation helps people decide what to do next. | **High.** Use a large “Payment verified” state with amount, time, and reference for the customer waiting in line. Keep the state backed by the server. |
| [Shopify: mobile checkout guidance](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/mobile-checkout) | Keep the mobile path short, make the next action clear, and explain field errors close to the field. | Less typing and fewer detours help customers who are using a phone while standing in a queue. | **High.** A single amount field and one primary action fit; avoid optional account or address steps. |
| [Shopify Help: one-page checkout](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/customize-checkout-configurations/one-page-checkout) | Keep related order and payment information together on one page when the task is small. | It reduces navigation between separate screens and keeps the current task visible. | **Partial.** BolPay has a short amount-entry/payment-wait flow; keep its steps focused rather than forcing a long checkout form into one page. |
| [Shopify Help: checkout styling](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/customize-checkout-configurations/checkout-style) | Favor readable contrast and restrained decoration on a task-oriented payment screen. | Visual emphasis stays on amount, payee, and action rather than competing imagery. | **High.** Useful for sunlight and small displays. Keep the payee and amount prominent without borrowing Shopify’s styling. |
| [Square: restaurant POS](https://squareup.com/us/en/point-of-sale/restaurants) | Reduce repeated order-entry steps and surface the common actions in the order-taking workspace. | Staff can complete routine actions without searching through nested screens during a rush. | **High.** Keep the vendor’s quick check and current queue visible, with large controls and little secondary text. |
| [Square: Kitchen Display System](https://squareup.com/us/en/point-of-sale/restaurants/kitchen-display-system) | Use live tickets, elapsed-time cues, and a clear completion action to manage work across a queue. | Staff can scan what is waiting and act on a ticket directly. | **High with adaptation.** Keep each payment row readable at a glance; use icon and word alongside color, and avoid a tiny dense kitchen-ticket layout. |
| [Toast Support: basic kitchen configuration](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Basic-Kitchen-Configuration?lang=en_US) | Separate an all-orders expediter view from station-specific work; order tickets progress in a visible queue. | A role-based view avoids showing each worker irrelevant detail while the expediter can oversee the whole queue. | **Partial.** BolPay vendors need one compact board, not kitchen routing. A summary count plus simple order rows is the useful part. |
| [Toast Support: KDS FAQ](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/KDS-FAQ) | Show a clear offline/resync state and explain a recovery action when the display loses connection. | Staff can distinguish a stale screen from an empty queue and know what to do. | **High.** A vendor board should visibly say when it cannot update and provide a refresh/reconnect path; never silently imply that the queue is current. |
| [Lightspeed: using KDS 2.0](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/22708154090267-Using-the-Kitchen-Display-System-2-0) | Keep status totals and ticket filters available; support a brief undo after an accidental completion. | A quick overview helps triage, while a short undo window reduces the cost of a mistaken tap. | **High with adaptation.** Counts and generous row targets suit quick vendor scanning. An undo is worth considering later, but is outside this preview scope. |
| [Lightspeed: Order Display Screen](https://k-series-support.lightspeedhq.com/hc/en-us/articles/4413390373403-Using-the-Order-Display-Screen) | Give waiting and ready orders distinct areas and keep a short pickup identifier visible. | Customers can see whether their order is ready without interpreting a detailed ticket. | **High.** A token is the appropriate queue identifier for BolPay. Use direct English/Hindi wording and show the vendor identity beside the amount on the customer screen. |

## Patterns applied to the preview

- **Customer in a queue:** show payee, amount, and one primary action; lock the amount after payment begins; use a prominent state with a time and reference after confirmation.
- **Vendor in noise and sunlight:** large counters and order values, high contrast, sparse row content, visible connection state, and status word plus a distinct icon. Voice can supplement the screen but cannot carry the only signal.
- **English and Hindi:** test the layout with both scripts, keep button labels sentence case, and allow lines to wrap without shrinking the type.
- **Low-end phones:** static local markup, limited animation, and no downloaded third-party media or font payload.

These patterns are design hypotheses drawn from product guidance, not usability findings from BolPay vendors or customers. Real vendor and customer testing remains outstanding.
